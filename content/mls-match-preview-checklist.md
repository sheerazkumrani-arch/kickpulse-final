Not every match preview is worth your time. Some are three paragraphs of filler wrapped around a score prediction. A good MLS preview does something different: it gets you ready for the game in two or three minutes, so you know what to look for when the whistle blows.

Here is a six-point checklist you can use on any preview, ours or anyone else's. If a preview misses more than two of these points, keep looking.

## 1. Kickoff time in your own time zone

MLS clubs play across several time zones in the United States and Canada, so a "7:30 pm" kickoff can mean very different things depending on where you live. For fans in Pakistan, the UK or Europe, that same match may be in the middle of the night. A good preview gives you the time in a way you can use, or at least makes it easy to convert. Our guide to [MLS kickoff times in Pakistan, the UK and Europe](/articles/mls-kickoff-times-pakistan-uk-europe/) shows how.

## 2. Recent form, home and away

Look for the last five results of both teams. But do not stop at the wins and losses. Ask whether the team was at home or away, and who it played. One win after a bad run tells you much less than a steady pattern over several weeks. Home form matters in MLS in particular, because travel distances are long and conditions change from city to city.

## 3. Team news and injuries

Injuries and suspensions change a match more than almost anything else. The best previews name the players who are out, say how doubtful the others are, and explain what difference it makes. "Their striker is out" is not enough. "Their striker is out, so they will probably play with a faster, smaller front line" is a preview doing its job.

A good preview also tells you where the news came from. If it says only "reports suggest", be careful. We cover how to find reliable news in our [guide to MLS team news, lineups and injuries](/articles/mls-team-news-lineups-injuries-guide/).

## 4. Predicted lineups

A predicted lineup is an educated guess, not a fact. Official lineups are usually announced about an hour before kickoff, so check again then. If a preview presents a predicted XI as if it were certain, treat it with caution. The most honest previews say which positions are almost certain and which are open.

## 5. The key matchup

Every game has a battle that could decide it. It might be a fast winger against a slow full-back, a young goalkeeper facing a team that shoots from distance, or a coach who likes to press against a team that likes to build from the back. A preview that picks one clear matchup and explains it is far more useful than one that lists ten.

## 6. Where to watch

Broadcast rights differ from country to country, and they change from season to season. Check the league's official site for the broadcaster in your region. If you cannot watch, you can still follow the game: the [MLS live scores](https://kickpulse-football.vercel.app/mls-live-scores/) page on KickPulse shows the scoreboard, goals and match status as they update.

## Use the preview, then follow the game

A preview is only half the story. Read it before kickoff, then switch to a live view once the match starts. ## A note about predictions

We sometimes include a score prediction at the end of a preview. Treat it as an opinion for fun, based on form and team news. It is not betting advice, and nobody, including us, can promise what happens on the pitch.

## Quick checklist to save

1. Kickoff time in your own time zone
2. Last five results, home and away
3. Injuries, suspensions and the source
4. Predicted lineups, and the official ones about an hour before kickoff
5. One key matchup
6. Where to watch in your country

Keep these six points in mind and you will quickly spot which previews are worth reading.
