A good MLS match preview saves you time and cuts through matchday noise. Instead of scrolling through dozens of social posts right before kickoff, you should be able to scan one clear article and know what actually matters. Before you settle in for any Major League Soccer fixture, check that the preview covers these six points.

## 1. Kickoff time in your own time zone

MLS clubs play across four North American time zones—Eastern, Central, Mountain, and Pacific—which makes fixture schedules confusing if you are watching from Europe, the UK, or South Asia. A 7:30 pm kickoff in New York starts at a very different hour from a 7:30 pm kickoff in Los Angeles or Seattle.

Always check which US time zone the listing uses and convert it to your local clock before planning your evening or setting an early alarm. If you need quick winter and summer conversions, use our [MLS kickoff times guide for Pakistan, the UK and Europe](/articles/mls-kickoff-times-pakistan-uk-europe/).

## 2. Recent form

Look at the last five results for both clubs, and pay special attention to **home versus away splits**. Many MLS teams dominate on their own turf in front of loud home crowds while struggling on long cross-country road trips.

One surprising win after a four-match losing streak tells you far less than a steady five-game pattern in goals scored, shots conceded, and defensive shape. A solid preview explains *why* a team is in form rather than just listing W-L-D letters.

## 3. Team news and injuries

Injuries, yellow-card suspensions, and international call-ups change an MLS match more than almost anything else. Because squad depth varies across the league under salary-cap rules, losing one Designated Player or starting centre-back can shift an entire game plan.

The best previews name the missing or doubtful players, cite official club availability reports, and explain which backup is likely to step in. For tips on verifying official updates yourself, see [where MLS lineups and injury updates come from](/articles/mls-team-news-lineups-injuries-guide/).

## 4. Predicted lineups

A predicted lineup is an educated projection based on recent games and training reports—not a confirmed fact. In Major League Soccer, official starting XIs and substitutes' benches are normally published roughly **60 minutes before kickoff**.

Use predicted lineups early in the day to understand each coach’s likely formation and tactical plan, then check the official team sheets an hour before kickoff to spot late rotation or surprise tactical changes.

## 5. The key matchup

Every match has one or two battles that tilt the 90 minutes. Good previews pick out a specific on-pitch duel—such as an overlapping wing-back pushing behind an aggressive winger, a mobile striker testing a high defensive line, or a creative number 10 operating against a single holding midfielder.

Understanding that single matchup makes the live match far more enjoyable to watch, because you immediately see where each coach is trying to create space.

## 6. Where to watch

Broadcast availability differs depending on where you live. Globally, **MLS Season Pass on Apple TV** streams every league match without local blackouts, while selected fixtures also appear on national TV channels such as **FOX / FS1** in the US or **TSN / RDS** in Canada. Always verify your regional listing before kickoff, and keep [MLS live scores on KickPulse](https://kickpulse-football.vercel.app/mls-live-scores/) open if you are following stats on the go.

## About predictions

Most match previews wrap up with a score prediction. Treat that pick as an editorial opinion for entertainment, built from recent form, travel load, and team news—never as guaranteed outcomes or betting advice.
