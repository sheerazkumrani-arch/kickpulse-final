Match overview

The 2026 MLS Cup Playoffs open with a proper Eastern Conference Wild Card showdown. No. 8 Orlando City host No. 9 FC Cincinnati on Thursday, 19 November at Inter&Co Stadium, with kickoff at 7:30 PM ET. It is a single match, so there is no second leg: if it is level after 90 minutes, extra time and penalties follow. The winner moves into the best-of-three Round One series against Nashville SC, which runs between 20 November and 2 December. This is also the first playoff meeting between the two clubs.

For Orlando, a win would be their first postseason victory since the 2024 Eastern Conference semi-final. For Cincinnati, defeat would mean missing the first round for the first time since Pat Noonan took charge in 2022, so the pressure is on both dressing rooms.

Form and head to head

The history is perfectly balanced. Across 15 earlier meetings, each side has won five and five have been drawn, and their most recent game in Orlando on 15 August finished 1-1. Cincinnati have not lost in their last four MLS meetings with Orlando.

The recent form tells a different story. Orlando ended the regular season by beating New England 1-0, while Cincinnati lost 2-0 at home to Nashville SC on Decision Day and finished ninth.

Orlando City preview

Orlando’s season looked shaky in the spring, then turned around in the late summer. Antoine Griezmann’s arrival is the big reason: nine of the club’s 13 league wins came after he joined. Martin Perelman’s side are unbeaten in their last seven home league games and score 1.9 goals per match.

The weakness is at the back. Orlando conceded 2.3 goals per game, one of the worst records in MLS, and last season they lost 3-1 at Chicago in the Wild Card match. A clean sheet is the safest route to a home win.

FC Cincinnati preview

Cincinnati are dangerous going forward, scoring 2.0 goals per game. Evander scored in double figures and set up 17 goals, level with Lionel Messi for second in the league. Tom Barlow played every one of the 34 regular-season games.

The concern is defence (2.2 goals conceded per game) and their playoff record on the road: they have never won a postseason away game inside 90 minutes. Pat Noonan has also never lost a match at Orlando’s stadium.

Team news and injuries

For Orlando, Griffin Dorsey is questionable with a back problem, while Tyrese Spicer and Robin Jansson are likely to miss out with foot injuries. Iago Teodoro is available again after suspension.

For Cincinnati, Julian Malatini is doubtful with a leg injury and Nick Hagglund is unlikely to clear concussion protocol. Obinna Nwobodo is suspended. These updates were reported on 17 November, so check the clubs’ official channels before kickoff.

Expected lineups

Orlando City (4-4-2): Crepeau; Taifi, Brekalo, Cartagena, Nogueira; Pasalic, B. Ojeda, Atuesta, Angulo; Griezmann, Dike.

FC Cincinnati (3-5-2): Celentano; Smith, Hadebe, Robinson; Martinez, Bucha, Evander, Anunga, Ramirez; Denkey, Barlow.

Key matchup

Watch Griezmann against Cincinnati’s back three. If Orlando find him between the lines, he can create chances for Dike. At the other end, Evander is the player who links Cincinnati’s midfield to Denkey and Barlow, and Orlando’s midfield will have to stop him turning with the ball.

The set pieces and the first goal may also decide the game. Orlando have won six straight home league games when scoring first, and they have never lost a home playoff match after taking the lead. If Cincinnati score first, though, their attack can punish a back line that has leaked goals all year.

Prediction

Both teams concede plenty and both score plenty, so goals look likely. Orlando have home advantage, the better recent record at Inter&Co Stadium and Griezmann in good form. Our prediction is Orlando City 2-1 FC Cincinnati, though a draw after 90 minutes would not be a surprise given how even the head to head is.
