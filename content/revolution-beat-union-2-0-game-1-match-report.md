# Revolution Beat Union 2–0 in Game 1: MLS Score, Own Goal Drama and Peyton Miller's Big Night

![Revolution beat Union 2–0 in Game 1 of the 2026 MLS Cup Playoffs at Gillette Stadium: Harriel own goal at 10 minutes, Peyton Miller goal at 73 minutes, Revs lead the series 1–0](/images/revs-beat-union-2-0-hero-banner.jpg)

**Quick answer:** The New England Revolution beat the Philadelphia Union 2–0 at Gillette Stadium on Saturday, November 21, 2026, in Game 1 of their Eastern Conference Round One series. Nathan Harriel's own goal in the 10th minute opened the scoring, and Peyton Miller added a second in the 73rd. The Revs lead the best-of-three series 1–0, and Game 2 is on Saturday, November 28, in Philadelphia.

## MLS Score Today: Revolution 2–0 Union (Final)

One cruel bounce. One cool finish. One shutout. That is the MLS score today, and it came in front of 18,084 fans on a warm afternoon at Gillette. It was New England's first playoff game since 2023, and the Revs treated it like a final.

## How the Own Goal Happened: A Cruel Bounce at 10'

The Revs made their first real push in the eighth minute. Two minutes later, they had the lead. Miller broke on the counter-attack and fired from the top of the box, Harriel stuck out a leg to block it, and the ball rolled past goalkeeper Andrew Rick into the far corner. It was New England's first own goal of the season and the first in a playoff game. Harriel's night got worse in the 17th minute, when he was booked for a tactical foul on Dor Turgeman.

## Why the Union's Plan Fell Short After the Early Goal

The Union started with an attack-minded lineup, with Quinn Sullivan in midfield over Indiana Vassilev. But chasing the game for nearly the whole match is hard. Reports said the Union lacked speed and movement in possession, so they could not break down New England's compact shape. They had 52% of the ball and 9 corners to 5, yet the Revs stayed organised and waited. Interim coach Ryan Richter used his bench to add attacking quality, but the urgency never fully arrived.

## Match Timeline: Own Goal, Posts and Near Misses

It was not for lack of trying. In the 23rd minute, Quinn Sullivan's cross hit Revs defender Ethan Kohler, and the ricochet missed the near post by a foot. In the 37th, Cavan Sullivan turned and hit a left-footer off the left post. After the break, Philadelphia pushed hard for 15 minutes and won three corners. In the 50th minute, Cavan Sullivan's 25-yard shot forced a leaping save from Matt Turner. Only 2 of the Union's shots were on target, and the Revs held firm.

## How the Revs Got the Second Goal

The turning point was a substitution. Jack Harrison came on in the second half and changed the game. In the 73rd minute he whipped a cross to the back post, where Miller was completely unmarked, and the 19-year-old finished for his first career playoff goal. Carles Gil was also credited with an assist. Miller became the fifth-youngest player to score in MLS Cup playoffs history, and the game was gone.

## Star Players: Miller, Harrison, Gil and Turner

**Peyton Miller** was the star. He created the first goal, scored the second, and had two shots on target and two key passes. The Connecticut native and Revs homegrown player called playing for his childhood club "surreal." He also won his duel with Cavan Sullivan, the Union's teenage star.

**Jack Harrison** earned Player of the Match from the bench. **Carles Gil** kept the Revs calm, and **Matt Turner** made two saves for his second career playoff shutout in MLS. For the Union, Cavan Sullivan was the most dangerous, Harriel was unlucky rather than poor, and Rick had no chance on the own goal.

## What the Revs Did Right

Coach Marko Mitrović, now the fifth Revs coach to manage a playoff game in his first season, said an early goal is great but a long game remains. His team listened. They stayed patient, defended the box and struck when Harrison gave them width. It is the same recipe that worked on October 24, when the Union outshot the Revs 24–12 and still lost 2–1.
